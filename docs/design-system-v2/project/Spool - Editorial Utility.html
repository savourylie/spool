SPL/2.0
Editorial Utility
Originally "Playful Geometric"

A quieter system for reading the algorithm.

Spool 2.0 trades the sticker-book for an editorial utility — one typeface in five weights, paper-and-ink contrast, monospace where structure matters, and a single muted accent that gets out of the way. Everything is sharp, hairline-bordered, and tabular by default.

F.01 · Color

Paper and ink, plus one accent.

The previous "confetti" palette has been collapsed to a high-contrast neutral foundation on warm paper. A single muted electric blue carries links, focus rings, and selections — never decorative fills. Semantic colors (positive / negative / warning) appear only inside data and banners.

F.02 · Type

One sans, five weights, mono for structure.

Geist replaces the Outfit / Plus Jakarta pairing. Headings use weight 500 with a tight negative track — sharp, not heavy. Geist Mono carries eyebrow labels, timestamps, tabular numerals, and any structural metadata where columns need to align.

F.04 · Radii

Architectural corners.

The system tops out at 8 pixels. The pill radius is reserved for avatars and selection chips — never buttons or cards. Sharp corners do the heavy lifting; small radii soften only what would otherwise feel brutal.

F.05 · Lines

Hairlines, not shadows.

The previous system depended on 4-pixel hard drop-shadows. This one depends on a single 1px hairline at three depths. Elevation is communicated through tone and weight, not lift.

C.01 · Buttons

Two intents, one shape.

Primary is ink on paper; everything else is hairlined. No gradient fills, no hard drop shadows, no inverted color states. The accent appears only in the focus ring.

C.02 · Inputs

Quiet at rest, ink-blue when focused.

One field shape. The focus state is the only place the accent appears — a 3px soft tint plus a 1px ink-blue border.

C.03 · Badges & status

Mono labels, semantic tints.

Every badge is a monospace, uppercase, tracked tag — never a colored pill. Status is communicated by tone, not saturation.

C.04 · Avatars

Initials in mono, on paper.

Avatars are paper-3 chips with mono initials. They acquire a portrait only when a real image is loaded. Square avatars are reserved for brand and project entities; round for people.

AT AT AT AT SP SP AT MK JR +9
C.05 · Cards & list items

Hairlined cards, no chrome.

Cards are flat: paper or card background, 1px hairline, 4px radius. Emphasis comes from internal hierarchy — eyebrow, title, body — not from a colored border or icon circle.

C.06 · Icons

1.5-stroke, square caps.

Geometric line icons drawn on a 24px grid. Square caps and miter joins reinforce the architectural feel. No fill weights — emphasis comes from grouping, not from a solid alternate.

C.07 · Banners

Quiet system messages.

Banners adopt the matching semantic tint at low saturation and lean on a small leading glyph. They never use a full-width colored bar, only an inset hairlined card.

P.01 · Conversation

Threaded by hairline.

Replies are connected by a 1px gutter line that runs from one avatar to the next. There is no indentation, no nested cards, no rounded "speech bubble" — only the line.

P.02 · Navigation

Sidebar of intent, tabs of context.

The sidebar lists what you do (verbs and nouns). Tabs underneath each surface partition the same data different ways. The active state is a filled ink rectangle in the sidebar and an underline in the tabs — never both styles in the same row.
